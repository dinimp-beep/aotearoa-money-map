      🏫 Schools in and near this area

      Examples of schools that serve this area — tap a name to see it on Google Maps. School zones are set by street address, so always check the zone for the exact home you're renting.

      How school zoning works

      • Many popular state schools have an enrolment zone. If you live in the zone, your child is guaranteed a place.
      • Out-of-zone places, if any, are usually decided by a ballot.
      • Schools ask for proof of address — a signed tenancy agreement usually counts — and some check that you really live there.
      • Some areas have full primary schools (Years 1–8) instead of a separate intermediate, and some secondary schools start at Year 7.

      What each type costs

      • State schools: free for domestic students. Schools ask for a voluntary donation plus costs for uniforms, trips and activities.
      • State-integrated: government-funded schools with a special character (often Catholic or Christian). Families pay compulsory attendance dues, usually a few hundred to about two thousand dollars a year.
      • Private (independent): fee-paying — often $20,000+ a year for secondary school.
      • Children of some temporary visa holders pay international fees even at state schools — see the Cost of Living tab.

      Check zones and school details with the official Education Counts school finder, then confirm with the school. School lists here are examples, not a complete list.

      Renting in NZ — the basics

      • Bond: landlords can ask for up to 4 weeks' rent, and it must be lodged with Tenancy Services — you get it back at the end if there's no damage or unpaid rent.
      • Rent in advance: usually 1–2 weeks, paid on top of the bond.
      • Healthy Homes standards: rentals must meet rules for heating, insulation, ventilation and moisture. Ask the landlord for the Healthy Homes compliance statement.
      • Furnished? Most NZ rentals are unfurnished but include an oven and often a dishwasher; fridges and washing machines aren't always included.
      • Power & internet are usually extra. Rooms and granny flats sometimes include them — ask.
      • Viewings: rentals go fast in Auckland and Wellington. Have references, ID and proof of income ready.

      About these numbers

      City-wide rents come from the Trade Me Rental Price Index and Tenancy Services bond data (2026). Auckland area differences use Barfoot & Thompson's August 2026 average 3-bedroom rents by suburb. Wellington and Christchurch area differences use relative median rents by suburb from the 2023 Census, scaled to today's city-wide rents. For Hamilton, Dunedin, Napier, Palmerston North and Invercargill, area differences are indicative. Property-type adjustments (apartment, townhouse, granny flat) are estimates. Ranges show where most listings fall; individual homes can sit well outside them. Always check current listings and the Tenancy Services market rent tool.

          Tax codes explained

          CodeWhen to use it
          MYour main or highest-paying job.
          MEMain job, income $24,000–$70,000 a year, and no Working for Families, NZ Super, benefit or Student Allowance — gives you the Independent Earner Tax Credit ($10 a week).
          SBSecond job, total income up to $15,600.
          SSecond job, total income $15,601–$53,500.
          SHSecond job, total income $53,501–$78,100.
          STSecond job, total income $78,101–$180,000.
          SASecond job, total income over $180,000.
          + SLAdd “SL” to any code if you have a student loan (e.g. M SL).
          NDNo declaration — if you don't give your employer an IRD number and tax code, tax is taken at 45%.
          WTSchedular payments — for some contractors (tax rate depends on the work).

          You give your tax code to your employer on the IR330 Tax code declaration form when you start a job.

          How to get an IRD number

          1. Check you can apply. You need to be in New Zealand (or arriving) on a visa that lets you work or study, or be a citizen or resident.
          2. Apply online at ird.govt.nz. New arrivals can use the “new arrival” application, which lets Inland Revenue check your identity with Immigration NZ. It's open until the latest arrival date on your visa — after that, use the “living in New Zealand” application with proof of identity.
          3. Have your documents ready: passport, visa details and contact details. Apply for your children at the same time if they need IRD numbers.
          4. Wait for your number: usually by text or email within 2 days, or by post within 10 days if approved.
          5. Give it to your employer and bank. Fill in the IR330 with your tax code, and give your IRD number to your bank so interest isn't taxed at the higher rate.
          6. Set up myIR. Register for a myIR account to see your income, refunds, student loan and KiwiSaver in one place.

          Applying is free. Never pay a third-party website to get an IRD number for you.

          About these numbers

          Income tax brackets: 10.5% up to $15,600, 17.5% to $53,500, 30% to $78,100, 33% to $180,000 and 39% above. ACC earners' levy 1.75% on income up to $156,641 (2026-27). IETC $520 a year for incomes $24,000–$66,000, reducing by 13 cents per dollar to zero at $70,000. Student loan repayments are 12% of income above $24,128. KiwiSaver employer contributions are taxed (ESCT) before reaching your account. This is an annual estimate: actual pay slips can differ slightly because of rounding and pay periods. Not tax advice.

            Ways to holiday for less in NZ

            • Campervan relocations — rental companies need vans moved between cities, sometimes from $1 a day and occasionally with the ferry included. Dates are fixed and one-way.
            • DOC campsites — basic sites are free and standard sites cost about $10–20 per adult a night; children under 5 stay free.
            • Book buses early — Intercity's cheapest fares sell out first; Auckland–Wellington is roughly $64–76 one way.
            • Travel off-peak — March–May and September–November are cheaper and quieter than summer and school holidays.
            • Free museums & walks — Te Papa is free for NZ residents, Auckland Domain is free for everyone, and most of NZ's best day walks cost nothing.
            • Cook and picnic — holiday park kitchens let a family eat for a fraction of café prices.
            • Holiday park memberships — Top 10 and similar clubs give around 10% off stays if you travel often.
            • Use Gaspy — compare fuel prices along your route; regional prices can differ a lot.

            Estimates use fuel at about $2.95/L, Cook Strait ferry from about $180 for a car and driver, DOC and holiday park fees, and typical cabin, hostel and motel rates. Prices change by season — always check before you book.
